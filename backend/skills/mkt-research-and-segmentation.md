## When to use
Sizing an opportunity, choosing which customers to serve, building personas, writing a research brief or questionnaire, or comparing competitors before positioning, GTM or budget work.

## Method
1. Decision first: name the decision the research informs and the action each plausible answer would trigger. Then pick secondary data, qualitative (8-15 interviews per segment, stop when themes repeat) or quantitative.
2. Segment (S of STP). Bases: firmographic (industry, size, region), demographic, geographic, behavioural (usage, occasion, loyalty), needs or jobs. Prefer needs and behaviour; demographics describe but rarely explain. Test each segment: measurable, substantial, accessible, differentiable, actionable. Cluster on needs variables (k-means, latent class), profile with demographics, check stability on a holdout.
3. Jobs to be done: job statement "When <situation>, I want to <motivation>, so I can <outcome>", with functional, social and emotional dimensions. Interview recent switchers about the timeline of their decision; map the four forces: push of the current situation, pull of the new, anxiety about the new, habit of the present. Competition is everything hired for the same job, including non-consumption.
4. Personas: one per priority segment, built from evidence (n interviews, survey share): job, triggers, buying role (user, economic buyer, champion, blocker), objections, channels, decision criteria. Label each attribute observed or assumed.
5. Target (T): weighted scoring of segments on size and growth, margin, competitive intensity, capability fit and cost to reach. Pick 1-3 and state who you will not serve.
6. Size: TAM = all buyers with the job x annual spend; SAM = share your product, geography and channels can serve; SOM = realistic capture in 3-5 years, ramp justified by sales capacity and comparables. Run top-down (reports x share) and bottom-up (accounts x penetration x ACV); reconcile to within about 30 % or explain the gap.
7. Competitors: direct, indirect, substitutes. Grid per rival: target segment, positioning claim, pricing and packaging, channels, strengths, weaknesses, recent moves. Add Porter five forces for industry attractiveness and a perceptual map on two buyer-chosen attributes.

## Survey design
- Proportion sample size (Cochran): n0 = z^2 x p(1-p) / e^2; z = 1.96 at 95 %, p = expected share (0.5 if unknown), e = margin of error. 95 %, +/-5 pp gives 385. Small population N: n = n0 / (1 + (n0 - 1) / N). Invites = completes / (response rate x incidence). Size every subgroup you will report (about 100+ per cell for directional reads). Margin of error at n: e = z x sqrt(p(1-p)/n). Weighting shrinks the sample: n_eff = n / deff, with Kish deff = 1 + CV^2 of the weights.
- One concept per question, neutral wording, balanced scales, randomised unordered options, forced choice instead of agree/disagree (acquiescence bias), general before specific questions, screeners first, demographics last, pretest with 5-10 cognitive interviews.

## Output
Decision and research questions; method and sample (n, frame, dates, weighting); segment table with size, needs and priority score; personas with evidence labels; TAM/SAM/SOM by both routes with an assumptions table; competitor grid; implications with confidence.

## Pitfalls
- TAM set to a whole industry's revenue; SOM with no capacity logic.
- Stated purchase intent read as demand (discount top-box intent).
- Opt-in panels reported as population estimates without weighting or caveat.
- Personas invented without data; segments nobody can reach.

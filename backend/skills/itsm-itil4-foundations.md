## When to use
Designing or assessing service management, explaining ITIL terms, mapping work onto practices and value streams, planning improvement.

## Version note
ITIL 4 (2019) is still widely used and certified; Managing Professional covers Create, Deliver and Support; Drive Stakeholder Value; High-velocity IT; Direct, Plan and Improve. PeopleCert launched ITIL (Version 5) in 2026 (verify the date locally): the service value system becomes the ITIL Value System; the value chain becomes the Product and Service Lifecycle Model; the seven principles and 34 practice names carry over; practices fall into two groups (product and service management, general management) as the technical group is dropped; an AI Capability Model of six Cs (creation, curation, clarification, cognition, communication, coordination) sits in the information and technology dimension. Use ITIL 4 terms unless the client has moved, then map names.

## Core concepts
- **Service**: a means of enabling value co-creation by facilitating outcomes customers want, without them managing specific costs and risks.
- **Value** is perceived by stakeholders and co-created by provider and consumer. Separate outputs from outcomes; weigh costs and risks removed against those imposed.
- **Utility** (fit for purpose) plus **warranty** (fit for use: availability, capacity, security, continuity).
- Roles: customer (defines requirements), user (uses), sponsor (funds).
- **Four dimensions**: organisations and people; information and technology; partners and suppliers; value streams and processes; shaped by external PESTLE factors.
- **SVS**: opportunity and demand become value through guiding principles, governance, the service value chain, practices and continual improvement.
- **Value chain activities**: plan, improve, engage, design and transition, obtain/build, deliver and support. A value stream strings activities together for one scenario (e.g. restore a user's service; ship a feature).
- **Guiding principles**: focus on value; start where you are; progress iteratively with feedback; collaborate and promote visibility; think and work holistically; keep it simple and practical; optimise and automate (optimise first).
- **Continual improvement model**: vision > where are we now > where do we want to be > how do we get there > take action > did we get there > keep momentum.

## The 34 practices
- General management (14): architecture, continual improvement, information security, knowledge, measurement and reporting, organisational change, portfolio, project, relationship, risk, service financial, strategy, supplier, workforce and talent.
- Service management (17): availability, business analysis, capacity and performance, change enablement, incident, IT asset, monitoring and event, problem, release, service catalogue, service configuration, service continuity, service design, service desk, service level, service request, service validation and testing.
- Technical management (3): deployment, infrastructure and platform, software development and management.

## Method
1. Name the value stream and its stakeholders; define outcomes and measures.
2. Baseline the current state from data.
3. Map steps to value chain activities and practices; find waits, handoffs and rework.
4. Change in small iterations; automate only after simplifying.
5. Output: value stream map (steps, waits, handoffs, rework), practice gaps, continual improvement register (item, owner, benefit, status).

## Pitfalls
- Treating a practice as a department or a single process; a practice includes people, information, tools and partners.
- Implementing all 34 practices at once.
- Measuring outputs (tickets closed) instead of outcomes.
- Confusing ITIL (guidance) with ISO/IEC 20000-1 (auditable requirements).

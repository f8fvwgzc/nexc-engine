## When to use
Writing or reviewing a policy, handling a complaint or misconduct allegation, a disciplinary, grievance or capability process, planning a dismissal, supporting wellbeing.

## Policies
Each policy: purpose, scope, definitions, rules, procedure, roles, reporting channels, non-retaliation, confidentiality and data handling, consequences, owner, version, review date, country notes. Core set: code of conduct, anti-harassment, equal opportunity, grievance, disciplinary, whistleblowing, leave, remote work, acceptable IT use, health and safety, employee privacy. EU Whistleblowing Directive 2019/1937: internal channel at 50+ workers, acknowledge within 7 days, feedback within 3 months.

## Fair investigation
1. Triage: risk, urgency, interim steps (separate parties; suspension on full pay only if needed, kept short, reviewed and never framed as a sanction), conflicts of interest.
2. Terms of reference: specific allegations (who, what, when, where), scope, an independent trained investigator, timeline.
3. Evidence: preserve records and logs; interview complainant, respondent and witnesses separately with open questions; agreed notes; the respondent sees the allegations and evidence and can answer.
4. Findings per allegation on the balance of probabilities, with reasons; weigh consistency, corroboration, plausibility.
5. Report: allegations, process, evidence, findings, recommendations. The investigator does not decide the sanction.
6. A separate decision maker; proportionate outcome; appeal to someone not involved before.
Need-to-know confidentiality; guard against retaliation; involve counsel early if litigation is likely.

## Disciplinary and grievance
- Write to the employee, meet, allow a companion (UK statutory right: colleague, trade union rep or official), decide in writing, offer appeal (Acas Code 2015; tribunals can adjust awards by up to 25% for unreasonable failure; a revised Code is in consultation).
- Ladder: informal, first written warning, final written warning, dismissal; gross misconduct can justify dismissal without notice after a fair process. Keep sanctions consistent.

## Jurisdiction caveats
| Place | Key points |
|---|---|
| US | at-will in most states (not Montana); limits: anti-discrimination law (Title VII, ADA, ADEA), retaliation, public policy, implied contract; NLRA protects concerted activity, union or not |
| EU | no at-will; national rules on notice and valid reasons; works council rights in many states; collective redundancy consultation (Directive 98/59/EC) |
| UK | Employment Rights Act 2025: unfair dismissal qualifying period 6 months (was 2 years) and no compensation cap from 1 Jan 2027; tribunal claim limit 6 months from 1 Oct 2026; all reasonable steps against sexual harassment, incl. by third parties, from 30 Oct 2026; fair reason plus fair procedure |
| Japan | Labor Contract Act Art 16: dismissal void without objectively reasonable grounds and social appropriateness (high bar); LSA Art 20: 30 days' notice or pay in lieu; LSA Art 89: rules of employment for 10+ workers; employers must take power-harassment prevention measures |

## Wellbeing
Assess psychosocial risks (ISO 45003), EAP, reasonable adjustments, return-to-work plans, manager training; treat health data as special category data.

## Records
Dated, factual, contemporaneous notes; letters stating allegations, evidence, companion right and possible outcomes; retention per policy.

## Output
Case plan, investigation report, outcome letter drafts, policy drafts with country notes, risk assessment.

## Pitfalls
Prejudging; one person investigating and deciding; delay; ignoring protected characteristics or whistleblower status; giving legal conclusions instead of referring to employment counsel.

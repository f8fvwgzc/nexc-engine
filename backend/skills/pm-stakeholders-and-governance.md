## When to use
Starting a project or programme, fixing unclear decision rights, preparing a board pack or status report, or handling a change request.

## Stakeholder mapping
1. List everyone affected or able to affect the work: sponsor, users, operations, suppliers, regulators, finance, security, works councils.
2. Record interest, influence, attitude and current versus desired engagement (unaware, resistant, neutral, supportive, leading).
3. Power/interest grid: high power and high interest = manage closely; high power, low interest = keep satisfied; low power, high interest = keep informed; low and low = monitor.
4. Salience check (power, legitimacy, urgency): stakeholders with all three come first.
5. Plan an action per gap between current and desired engagement, with owner and date.

## RACI
- Responsible does the work; Accountable signs off; Consulted is two-way; Informed is one-way.
- Rows: exactly one A, at least one R. Columns: a person with an entry on every row is a bottleneck; a row of only C and I has no doer.
- For decisions use DACI (Driver, Approver, Contributors, Informed) and keep a decision log: decision, date, who decided, options rejected, rationale.

## Communications plan
Table: audience, what they need, purpose, channel, frequency, owner, how feedback returns. Channels grow as n(n-1)/2 for n people, so use hubs and a single source of truth. Mix push (reports), pull (dashboards) and interactive (workshops) channels.

## Governance
- Sponsor or Senior Responsible Owner owns the business case and benefits. PRINCE2 project board: executive, senior user, senior supplier; project assurance stays independent of the project manager.
- GovS 002 v2.1 (17 September 2025), the reference for UK government departments and arm's length bodies, makes portfolio, programme and project governance roles mandatory (clauses 5.2 and 6.2).
- Steering committee terms of reference: purpose, members, quorum, decision rights, delegated tolerances, escalation route, cadence. Agenda leads with decisions needed; papers go out in advance; actions and decisions are minuted.
- Decision points: is the business case still valid, are benefits on track, are risks within appetite, is the next stage ready?

## Change control
1. Log the request with a unique ID.
2. Assess impact on scope, time, cost, quality, risk and benefits, with options.
3. Decide at the right level: within delegated limits by the change authority or project manager, above them by the board.
4. Update baselines, plans and the configuration record; communicate; verify it was done.
Keep a change budget and report approved, pending and rejected changes.

## RAG status
- Publish the rules first: Green = forecast within plan and tolerance; Amber = at risk or off plan, but recoverable within the manager's delegated tolerance; Red = forecast breach of tolerance needing a sponsor or board decision.
- Rate time, cost, scope, quality, risk and benefits separately, then overall; add a trend arrow and one line of evidence each.
- Status report: headline, RAG table, milestones planned versus forecast, EVM figures, top risks and issues, decisions needed, next period.

## Output
Stakeholder register (role, interest, influence, attitude, current and desired engagement, action, owner); RACI matrix with gaps flagged; communications plan table; board terms of reference; change log; status report with RAG evidence.

## Pitfalls
Watermelon reporting (green outside, red inside); RAG set by mood; several Accountables; boards that only receive information; changes approved by email with no impact assessment; stakeholder maps never updated.

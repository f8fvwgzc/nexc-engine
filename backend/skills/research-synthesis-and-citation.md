## When to use
After search and source evaluation, to state what the evidence says, how sure you are, and where it came from.

## Method
1. Evidence table: one row per finding per source: finding, source, date, design or type, population or scope, result with units, tier, notes. Synthesise across rows, never one source at a time.
2. Triangulate: confirm key findings by independent data sources, by different methods (survey vs transaction data), and by a second reviewer where stakes are high. Sources that share an origin count once.
3. Reconcile disagreement. Report each value with its source and date, then diagnose: definition, scope or geography, period, method, currency or units, sample. Give a range, or a preferred value with the reason. Never silently average incompatible figures.
4. Grade confidence for each key finding (GRADE-style):
   - Start high for randomised or controlled experiments and audited records; start low for observational, case-study or self-reported evidence (GRADE: trials start high, observational studies low). Judge indirectness only once, as a reason to rate down.
   - Rate down one or two levels for: risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness (different population, setting or metric), imprecision (wide ranges, few data points, interval crossing the decision threshold), publication or reporting bias (only success stories, vendor-funded studies).
   - Rate up, rarely, for a large effect, a dose-response gradient, or when plausible bias would have shrunk the effect.
   - Levels: high (the true value is very probably near the estimate), moderate (probably near it, but could differ materially), low (may differ materially), very low (probably differs materially).
   For qualitative findings use GRADE-CERQual's four components: methodological limitations, coherence, adequacy of data, relevance.
5. Write findings as claims with a confidence and citation, e.g. "Unit costs fell 12-15 % in 2025 [S2, S5] (moderate: two sources, one vendor)."

## Citation formats
Choose one style per deliverable and apply it everywhere.
- Workspace: [S3] in text; reference list entry: S3. Owner, title, path/to/file.md, section or page, date of document.
- APA 7th: Author, A. A. (2025). Title of report. Publisher. URL
- Chicago 18th author-date: (Author 2025, 14) in text; Author. 2025. Title. Publisher.
- IEEE: [3] in text; [3] A. Author, "Title," Publisher, 2025.
Quote exactly, mark omissions with an ellipsis, give a page or section for every quote and figure.

## Output
Bottom line with confidence, key findings table (finding, confidence, sources), disagreements and how resolved, gaps, evidence table (appendix), references.

## Checklist
- Each key finding has a confidence grade and at least one citation.
- Disagreements shown, not hidden.
- No reference without an in-text use and none in text without a reference.

## Pitfalls
- Vote counting: five weak sources do not outweigh one strong one.
- Double-counting non-independent sources.
- Cherry-picking rows that fit the story.
- Confidence higher than the weakest critical link supports.
- Citation drift: the cited page does not actually say it.

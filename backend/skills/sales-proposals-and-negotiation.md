## When to use
Writing proposals, business cases and ROI models, building mutual action plans, handling objections and preparing a negotiation.

## Value-based business case
1. Anchor on the buyer's metrics from discovery: revenue up, cost down, risk down, cash released.
2. Quantify with the buyer's own figures; name an owner on their side for each assumption.
3. Formulas (horizon usually 3 years):
   - ROI = (total benefits - total costs) / total costs.
   - Payback (months) = upfront investment / monthly net benefit.
   - NPV = sum over t of net benefit_t / (1 + r)^t - initial cost, with r the buyer's hurdle rate.
   - Cost of delay = monthly net benefit x months of delay.
   - Example: upfront cost 300k, net benefit 250k a year for 3 years: ROI = (750 - 300) / 300 = 150%; payback = 300 / (250 / 12) = 14.4 months.
4. Show a conservative case (50-70% of expected benefit) and a sensitivity table; buyers discount vendor ROI.

## Proposal structure
Executive summary in the buyer's words (problem, outcome, investment, recommendation); current state and cost of inaction; solution mapped to their decision criteria; implementation plan and timeline; success metrics; options (good, better, best); terms and assumptions; proof (references, case studies); next steps. Send it only after the economic buyer has agreed scope; a proposal should hold no surprises.

## Mutual action plan
A shared, dated plan from today to go-live: business case sign-off, security review, legal redlines, procurement, signature, kick-off, each with an owner on both sides and a status. Work back from the buyer's compelling event. A buyer who will not co-own the plan is a forecast risk.

## Objection handling
Listen, acknowledge, explore (find the real concern; ask "compared with what?"), respond with evidence, confirm it is resolved. Price: reframe to value and cost of inaction. Timing or status quo: cost of delay. Competitor: decision criteria. Risk: pilot, references, SLAs. Authority: ask who else must agree and how to involve them.

## Negotiation
- Prepare: your BATNA and your estimate of theirs, translated into the same terms as the offer; walk-away point; target; ZOPA, the range between both walk-away points; every tradeable variable.
- Principled negotiation (Getting to Yes): separate people from the problem; interests, not positions; options for mutual gain; objective criteria such as benchmarks and list prices.
- Give-gets: never concede without a get. Gets: longer term, upfront payment, volume, reference or case study, signature by a date. Make each concession smaller and slower than the last; trade items cheap for you and valuable to them.
- Discount discipline: an approval matrix by discount band, deal desk review, a concessions log.
- Anchor first when well informed, and justify the anchor.

## Output
Business case with assumptions table, proposal, mutual action plan table, negotiation plan (BATNA, walk-away, target, give-get list, concessions log).

## Pitfalls
Discounting before value is agreed; feature dumps; ROI built on vendor-invented numbers; negotiating with someone who cannot sign; splitting the difference by reflex.

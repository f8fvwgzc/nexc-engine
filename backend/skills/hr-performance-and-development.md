## When to use
Setting goals, designing a review cycle, writing reviews, running calibration, building development plans, talent reviews, succession, improvement plans.

## Goals
- Specific, stretching objectives for well-defined work; learning or team goals for complex or novel work. Few, relevant measures: overweighted targets distort behaviour.
- OKRs: one qualitative objective with 2-5 measurable key results (outcomes, not tasks), usually quarterly. Score 0.0-1.0; about 0.6-0.7 is healthy for aspirational OKRs, committed ones should reach 1.0. Keep OKR scores out of pay formulas to avoid sandbagging.
- Align upward (link to a parent goal) rather than copying goals down; 3-5 goals per person.

## Continuous feedback
- Check-ins every 1-4 weeks: progress, blockers, support, growth.
- SBI feedback: Situation, Behaviour, Impact, then the ask; add feedforward on what to do next.
- Both sides keep an evidence log to counter recency bias.

## Reviews and calibration
- Rate against behaviourally anchored standards on a 3-5 point scale; assess results ("what") and behaviours ("how") separately.
- Calibration: pre-read with ratings and evidence; HR facilitator tests consistency across managers; discuss outliers and borderline cases; challenge with evidence; check distributions by gender, ethnicity, age band, part-time and leave status; log every change and reason.
- Rating parity = share of a group rated top / share of the highest group; below 0.80, or a significant chi-square across rating levels, means reopen those cases with evidence before sign-off. No forced ranking; guidance ranges only as a prompt.
- Rater errors: recency, halo or horns, leniency or severity, central tendency, similarity, idiosyncratic rater effect.
- Underperformance: diagnose (clarity, capability, capacity, motivation, health); informal support first; then an improvement plan with measurable standards, support, review dates (often 30-90 days) and recorded outcomes, aligned to the capability policy and local law. UK: a fair capability process (warning, real chance to improve) comes before any dismissal; Japan: low performance alone rarely makes a dismissal valid (Labor Contract Act Art 16) without coaching, retraining or redeployment first.

## Development (70-20-10)
- 70 challenging experience (stretch projects, rotations, bigger scope), 20 relationships (mentors, coaches, peers, the boss), 10 formal learning, which amplifies the rest. CCL research heuristic, not a quota; pick experiences that teach the lesson needed.
- IDP: aspiration, 2-3 goals tied to competencies, actions in each 70/20/10 bucket, support, success measures, dates; review quarterly.

## Succession
1. Critical roles: impact x scarcity x vacancy risk.
2. Success profile per role.
3. Talent review on a 9-box: performance (low, medium, high) x potential (low, medium, high); judge potential on evidence (learning agility, aspiration, ability), not tenure or likeness.
4. Successors by readiness: now, 1-2 years, 3-5 years; aim for 2 per critical role. Bench strength = critical roles with a ready-now successor / all critical roles.
5. Development actions, retention risk, emergency cover.

## Output
Cycle calendar and roles, OKR sheet, review template (goals, evidence, what and how ratings, development notes), calibration pack with parity table, IDP template, succession table (role, risk, successors, readiness, actions).

## Pitfalls
Annual-only feedback; ratings without evidence; potential judged by similarity; bonus tied to OKR scores; 9-box labels shared with the people rated.

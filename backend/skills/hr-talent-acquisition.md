## When to use
Opening a role, writing a job description, designing an interview loop, judging candidates, auditing a process for bias, making an offer, planning onboarding.

## Method
1. Job analysis: talk to the hiring manager and top incumbents; list 6-12 month outcomes, critical tasks and KSAOs (knowledge, skills, abilities, other). Split must-haves from trainable; drop proxies (degree, years) that are not job-related.
2. Competencies: 4-6, each with behavioural indicators per level.
3. Assessment plan: map every competency to at least one method. Stronger predictors for most jobs: structured interviews, job-knowledge tests, work samples, cognitive tests (watch adverse impact). Weaker: unstructured interviews, years of experience, references. Sackett et al. (2022) put structured interviews at or near the top of mean validity (verify figures before quoting).
4. Structured interview: same questions, same order; behavioural ("Tell me about a time...", probe with STAR) and situational ("What would you do if..."); 1-5 scale with anchored example answers per level; note evidence, not impressions; rate each competency alone before any debrief; 2-3 trained interviewers.
5. Decision: neutral chair; each interviewer gives scores and evidence before hearing others; compare to the bar, not to each other; record the reason.
6. Offer: inside the band; check internal equity (peer compa-ratios); written offer with title, level, pay, start date and lawful contingencies (right to work, checks).
7. Onboarding: pre-boarding (kit, accounts), day one, 30-60-90 plan with goals, buddy, weekly manager check-ins; track time to productivity and 90-day retention.

## Fairness checks
- Same criteria for all; remove names, photos and age signals at screening; diverse panels; interviewer calibration.
- Impact ratio = selection rate of a group / selection rate of the highest group. Below 0.80 signals possible adverse impact (29 CFR 1607.4(D)). Test significance (Fisher exact test for small samples, otherwise chi-square or a two-proportion z test); then check job-relatedness and less-discriminatory alternatives.
- Reasonable adjustments for disability; accessible formats.
- AI screening tools: high-risk under EU AI Act Annex III from 2 Dec 2027 (deployers inform candidates, workers and their representatives, and keep human oversight); GDPR Art 22 limits solely automated decisions; New York City Local Law 144 needs a yearly independent bias audit and candidate notice for automated employment decision tools.
- EU Pay Transparency Directive 2023/970 (transpose by 7 June 2026; check national law): give the pay range before interview, do not ask pay history.

## Checklist
- Every criterion traces to the job analysis; every question maps to one competency.
- Anchors for levels 1, 3 and 5 written before the first interview.
- Interviewers trained; independent scores logged before the debrief.
- Adjustments offered at invitation; same time limits and materials for all.
- Pass-through rates logged per stage and group, with impact ratio and significance test.
- Offer inside the band, or the exception and approver recorded.
- Privacy notice given; candidate data deleted at the end of the retention period.

## Metrics
Time to fill, time to hire, offer acceptance, quality of hire (12-month performance and retention), source yield, candidate experience score, stage pass-through rates by group.

## Output
Job analysis summary, competency-to-method matrix, question bank with anchored rubrics, scorecard, adverse-impact table, offer recommendation, onboarding plan.

## Pitfalls
"Culture fit" as a veto; halo effect; unlawful questions (age, pregnancy, religion, marital status); loops that differ per candidate; keeping candidate data past the retention period.

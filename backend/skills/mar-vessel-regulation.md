## When to use
Vetting a ship for charter or purchase, preparing for port state control, writing a compliance plan, or pricing emissions into a voyage. Flag and class rules add to the conventions: name the flag, and leave certification to the flag state and recognised organisation.

## Safety, security, labour
- SOLAS: II-1 and II-2 construction and fire, III life-saving, IV radio, V navigation (masters report lost or drifting containers from 1 Jan 2026), VI cargo and VGM, VII dangerous goods, IX ISM, XI-2 ISPS, XIV Polar Code.
- ISM Code: company safety management system, designated person ashore, Document of Compliance (company), Safety Management Certificate (ship), internal audits, non-conformity closure.
- ISPS Code (since 1 July 2004): ship security plan, SSO, CSO and PFSO, security levels 1-3, International Ship Security Certificate.
- MLC 2006 (113 ratifications): employment terms, hours of rest, wages, accommodation, medical care, complaints; ships of 500 GT and over on international voyages carry an MLC certificate with DMLC Parts I and II. Amended up to 2025; check what the flag applies.

## MARPOL Annex VI
- Sulphur: 0.50 percent m/m outside ECAs since 1 Jan 2020; 0.10 percent inside ECAs (Baltic, North Sea, North American, US Caribbean, Mediterranean from 1 May 2025; Canadian Arctic and Norwegian Sea from 1 March 2027). Carriage ban on non-compliant fuel since 1 March 2020 unless a scrubber is fitted. A FONAR is not an exemption.
- EEXI (400 GT and over): attained index at or below required, usually met by engine power limitation.
- CII (5,000 GT and over): attained = annual CO2 / (capacity x distance sailed); rating A-E; D three years running or E once requires a corrective action plan in the SEEMP (Part III). Required reduction vs 2019: 5, 7, 9, 11 percent for 2023-2026; 13.625, 16.25, 18.875 and 21.5 percent for 2027-2030 (resolution MEPC.400(83); verify the yearly values locally).
- IMO Net-Zero Framework: adoption was adjourned in October 2025 for one year; check the outcome of the resumed session (October 2026, verify locally). 2023 strategy vs 2008: at least 20 percent cut by 2030, 70 percent by 2040, net zero by or around 2050.

## EU rules (5,000 GT and over, any flag)
- EU ETS: 100 percent of emissions on intra-EU voyages and at EU berths, 50 percent on voyages into or out of the EU. Surrender 40 percent of 2024 emissions, 70 percent of 2025, 100 percent from 2026; methane and N2O from 2026; due by 30 September of the following year. Cost = CO2e in scope x phase-in x EUA price.
- FuelEU Maritime (Regulation 2023/1805, from 1 Jan 2025): well-to-wake GHG intensity below 91.16 gCO2e/MJ by 2 percent (2025), 6 (2030), 14.5 (2035), 31 (2040), 62 (2045), 80 (2050). Energy in scope as for ETS (100 percent intra-EU and at berth, 50 percent to or from third countries).
- FuelEU maths: target 2025-2029 = 91.16 x 0.98 = 89.34 gCO2e/MJ; compliance balance CB = (target - actual) x energy in scope (MJ); penalty = |CB| / (actual x 41,000) x EUR 2,400, times 1 + (n - 1)/10 for the n-th consecutive deficit year; banking, borrowing and pooling; shore power for container and passenger ships in major ports from 2030; FuelEU document of compliance by 30 June.
- Liable party for both: the shipping company holding the ISM DOC; charter clauses may pass costs on.

## Pitfalls
- Treating a C rating as safe while factors tighten yearly.
- Forgetting the at-berth share and the extra-EU 50 percent in ETS.
- Vetting on certificates alone without recent PSC detentions and deficiencies.

## When to use
Classifying, valuing or claiming preference for goods, or deciding whether a licence or sanctions hit blocks a shipment. The law is national: name the country and as-of date; binding positions go to a licensed customs broker or trade counsel.

## Classification
- HS 2022 is in force; HS 2028 applies from 1 January 2028 (use WCO correlation tables). Six digits are global; national lines extend them (US HTSUS 10, EU CN 8 and TARIC 10, Japan 9).
- General Rules of Interpretation, in order: GRI 1 heading texts with section and chapter notes; 2(a) incomplete or unassembled goods having essential character, 2(b) mixtures; 3 when two headings fit: (a) most specific, (b) essential character for mixtures and sets, (c) last in numerical order; 4 most akin; 5 cases and packing; 6 subheadings compared only at the same level.
- Record the Explanatory Notes used and any binding ruling (EU BTI, US CBP ruling, Japan advance ruling).

## Valuation (WTO Agreement)
- Transaction value: price paid or payable plus Article 8 additions: selling commissions, packing, assists, royalties tied to the goods, resale proceeds to the seller, and freight and insurance to the border in CIF-basis countries (EU, Japan; the US uses FOB). Exclude buying commissions and post-import costs.
- Related parties (Art. 15 defines them): price stands if the relationship did not influence it or a test value is met.
- Fallbacks in strict order: identical goods, similar goods, deductive, computed, reasonable means; the importer may ask to swap deductive and computed.

## Origin
- Find the agreement and the product-specific rule for the HS line; test wholly obtained, tariff shift (CC, CTH, CTSH), regional value content or specific process; apply cumulation and tolerance; check minimal-operations and direct-transport rules; keep evidence.
- EU-Japan EPA (since 1 Feb 2019): exporter statement on origin (REX number in the EU) or importer's knowledge; 10 percent value tolerance. RCEP (since 2022): certificate or declaration of origin, RVC 40 or CTH on many lines. CPTPP: certification by importer, exporter or producer.
- AEO (WCO SAFE Framework, 2025 edition): trusted-trader status, recognised abroad through MRAs.

## Export controls and sanctions
- US EAR (15 CFR 730-774): ECCN on the CCL or EAR99, Country Chart, part 744 end-use and end-user rules (Entity List), de minimis and foreign direct product rules, then part 740 licence exceptions. The BIS Affiliates Rule (Entity List reach to firms 50 percent or more owned by listed parties) was suspended until 9 November 2026: check whether it now applies (verify locally).
- EU Regulation 2021/821: Annex I list updated yearly (latest delegated act adopted 14 September 2026), catch-all for non-listed items (Art. 4), cyber-surveillance (Art. 5); general, global and individual authorisations.
- Japan FEFTA: list control under the Export Trade Control Order and Foreign Exchange Order, catch-all control, METI End User List.
- Screen every party (buyer, consignee, end user, banks, vessel by IMO number) against OFAC, EU, UK and UN lists. OFAC 50 Percent Rule: blocked persons owning 50 percent or more in aggregate block the entity; control alone does not, but EU and UK tests include control.

## Output
Classification memo (facts, candidate headings, GRI path, national code, rulings); valuation build-up; origin test against the named rule with evidence list; screening log (party, list, date, result); red flags and the licence or ruling still needed.

## Pitfalls
- Classifying by use or supplier code; skipping chapter notes.
- Claiming preference without origin proof; omitting assists or royalties.
- Screening only the buyer; clearing red flags (odd routing, vague end use, cash) without escalation.

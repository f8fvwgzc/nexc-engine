## When to use
Turning test results into findings, rating them, drafting or reviewing an audit report, agreeing action plans, or running follow-up and closure.

## Anatomy of a finding (5 Cs)
- Condition: what is happening, from evidence; quantify (e.g. 6 of 25 samples, value affected, period).
- Criteria: what should happen: policy, law, contract, framework or agreed good practice (the evaluation criteria set at planning, Standard 13.4). A gap between criteria and condition is a potential finding (14.2).
- Cause: the root cause, identified with management where possible (14.3; 5 whys, fishbone): people, process, technology, governance. Symptoms are not causes.
- Effect: actual or potential consequence (financial, regulatory, operational, customer, reputational), with likelihood and impact.
- Recommendation or action: closes the criteria-condition gap, brings risk to an acceptable level and addresses the root cause (14.4); proportionate, discussed with management, and leaving detailed design to them.

## Rating
Use the CAE's single methodology for every engagement (14.3); weigh impact, likelihood, risk tolerance, pervasiveness, fraud indicators and repeat status. Example scale:
| Rating | Meaning | Typical target |
|---|---|---|
| High | Key control missing or failing, regulatory breach or possible material loss | 30-90 days, board visibility |
| Medium | Weakness with moderate exposure | within 6 months |
| Low | Improvement opportunity | within 12 months |
Engagement conclusion (14.5): overall judgement on governance, risk management and control, e.g. effective, needs improvement, ineffective, and say what works well.

## Management action plans
Each action is specific, owned by a named role, dated, with interim mitigation and the evidence that will prove completion. If auditors and management disagree, follow the documented resolution method (14.4). If management accepts risk beyond appetite, discuss with senior management and escalate to the board if unresolved (11.5).

## Follow-up
Per 15.2: track every action in an issue register, ask for progress, and do risk-based follow-up work (e.g. quarterly status, retest high-rated items, close only on evidence). If an action is late, get and document management's explanation and take it to the CAE, who decides whether senior management has in effect accepted risk beyond tolerance (then 11.5 applies). Report overdue items and extensions to the audit committee.

## Report structure (15.1)
1. Title, distribution list, date.
2. Executive summary: objective, overall conclusion or rating, top findings, positive observations.
3. Background, scope, period and any scope limitations.
4. Findings by rating: title, rating, condition, criteria, cause, effect, recommendation, management response, owner, due date; acknowledge actions already taken.
5. Appendices: rating definitions, method, sample details.
6. If the engagement did not conform with the Standards, say which requirements were missed, why, and the effect on results (15.1).
The CAE reviews and approves before issue; the text must be accurate, objective, clear, concise, constructive, complete and timely (11.2).

## Pitfalls
Reporting symptoms; missing criteria; effects not backed by evidence; vague actions without owners or dates; recommendations that dictate a solution; surprising management with findings in the final report.

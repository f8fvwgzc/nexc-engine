## When to use
A feature, vendor, HR or marketing use of personal data; DSARs; transfers; breaches; privacy notices. Name the regimes: controller establishment, where data subjects are, sector.

## GDPR
UK GDPR is close but amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (recognised legitimate interests, wider automated decisions, complaints to the controller first; phased, verify locally).
- Art 5 principles: lawfulness, fairness, transparency; purpose limitation; minimisation; accuracy; storage limitation; security; accountability.
- Art 6(1) bases: (a) consent (specific, informed, unambiguous, withdrawable), (b) contract, (c) legal obligation, (d) vital interests, (e) public task, (f) legitimate interests with a documented test: purpose, necessity, balancing. Employee consent is rarely free.
- Art 9 special data also needs an Art 9(2) condition.
- Art 30 records; Art 28 processor contract; Art 32 security; Art 25 privacy by design and default (minimise, pseudonymise, sharing off by default, retention schedule).
- Rights Arts 15-22 (access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, Art 22 automated decisions). Answer within one month, plus two months if complex (Art 12(3)); verify identity proportionately; free unless manifestly unfounded or excessive.
- DPIA (Art 35) for likely high risk: large-scale special data, systematic monitoring, profiling with significant effects; two or more EDPB criteria usually means do one. Contents: description, necessity and proportionality, risks to individuals, measures. Residual high risk: Art 36 prior consultation.
- Breach: clock starts when the controller (any staff) is reasonably certain; notify the competent or lead authority within 72 hours unless unlikely to pose risk (Art 33), in phases if needed; processors tell the controller without undue delay; tell individuals without undue delay if high risk (Art 34), e.g. exposed passwords; log all breaches (Art 33(5)).
- Art 83 fines: up to EUR 10m or 2%, or 20m or 4% of worldwide turnover, if higher.

## Transfers (Chapter V)
1. Adequacy (Art 45), e.g. Japan, UK, and US organisations certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework (decision of 10 July 2023); check the importer's live certification. The General Court upheld the DPF in September 2025 (appeal: verify locally).
2. Art 46 safeguards: SCCs (Decision 2021/914; modules C2C, C2P, P2P, P2C) or BCRs, with a transfer impact assessment and supplementary measures.
3. Art 49 derogations only for occasional transfers.

## Japan APPI
Specify and notify purposes; consent for third-party provision (Art 27; opt-out route not for sensitive data); foreign recipients need consent after telling the person about the country's regime, unless the country is PPC-designated (EU, UK) or the recipient keeps equivalent measures by contract with monitoring (Art 28); leaks likely to harm rights go to the PPC and the individuals (Art 26), preliminary report promptly (about 3-5 days) and final within 30 days (60 for wrongful acts); disclosure requests (Art 33). Pending amendments: verify locally.

## CCPA as amended by CPRA
Thresholds: revenue over USD 25m (CPI-adjusted), 100,000+ consumers, or 50%+ revenue from selling or sharing. Rights: know, delete, correct, opt out of sale or sharing (honour Global Privacy Control), limit sensitive data; respond in 45 days (+45). CPPA rules effective 1 Jan 2026: risk assessments now (attestation by 1 Apr 2028), ADMT duties from 1 Jan 2027, audit certifications from 1 Apr 2028 by revenue tier.

## Output
Processing summary; roles; basis per purpose; data map; risk table; transfer route; DSAR or breach timeline with clock start; open items.

## Pitfalls
Consent as default; missing sub-processors; late 72-hour clock; calling pseudonymised data anonymous.

## When to use
Building or reviewing a compliance programme, preparing board reporting, scoping an anti-bribery or anti-fraud framework, or answering "would this programme stand up to a regulator or prosecutor?"

## Frameworks
- DOJ ECCP (updated September 2024), three questions:
  1. Well designed? Risk assessment (incl. AI and new technology), policies and procedures, training and communications, confidential reporting and investigation, third-party management, M&A.
  2. Adequately resourced and empowered? Senior and middle management commitment, autonomy and resources (incl. data access), compensation and consequence management (clawbacks).
  3. Works in practice? Continuous improvement, periodic testing and review; investigation of misconduct; root-cause analysis and remediation.
- US Sentencing Guidelines section 8B2.1: elements of an effective programme.
- IIA Three Lines Model (2020): governing body (oversight, accountability); management in first-line (own and manage risk) and second-line roles (expertise, monitoring, challenge; compliance sits here); internal audit as third line (independent assurance). Audit stays independent yet works closely with management.
- FCPA: anti-bribery (corrupt offers to foreign officials to win or keep business) plus books-and-records and internal-controls rules for issuers. DOJ's June 2025 guidelines narrowed enforcement priorities; the statute is unchanged.
- UK Bribery Act 2010: s.1 bribing, s.2 being bribed, s.6 foreign public officials, s.7 failure to prevent bribery with an adequate-procedures defence; no facilitation-payment exception. Ministry of Justice six principles: proportionate procedures, top-level commitment, risk assessment, due diligence, communication and training, monitoring and review.
- UK ECCTA 2023 failure to prevent fraud, in force 1 September 2025 for large organisations (two of: over 250 staff, over GBP 36m turnover, over GBP 18m assets); defence: reasonable prevention procedures, built on the guidance's six principles (top-level commitment, risk assessment, proportionate procedures, due diligence, communication and training, monitoring and review).
- Whistleblowing: Directive (EU) 2019/1937, internal channels for employers with 50 or more workers (acknowledge within 7 days, feedback within 3 months); confidential channels; no retaliation.
- Management-system benchmark: ISO 37301:2021 (compliance management systems).

## Rating scale
Rate each ECCP topic: 1 absent; 2 documented only; 3 implemented with evidence of use; 4 tested, measured and improved. Evidence beats policy text: hotline data, training test results, discipline records, testing reports, budgets, reporting lines.

## Method
1. Risk assessment: obligations register; inherent risk x control effectiveness = residual; refresh yearly and on triggers.
2. Policies: code of conduct plus topic policies, each with owner, version, review date; procedures make them operable.
3. Training: by role and risk; track completion; test effectiveness.
4. Monitoring and testing: annual plan, KRIs, analytics, control tests; issues log with owners and due dates.
5. Regulatory change: horizon scan, impact and gap analysis, implementation plan, sign-off.
6. Board reporting at least quarterly: metrics, breaches, regulator contact, resourcing.
7. Investigations: triage, privilege via counsel, root cause, consistent discipline, lessons learned.

## Output
Assessment report: scope and method, ECCP mapping table (question, evidence, rating, gap, action), heat map, remediation plan with owners and dates.

## Pitfalls
Paper programmes; compliance reporting to the business it checks; no metrics; ignoring third parties. Name the jurisdiction and leave legal opinions to counsel.

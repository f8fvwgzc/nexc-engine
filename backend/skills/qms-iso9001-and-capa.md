## Standard status
ISO 9001:2015 with Amendment 1:2024 (climate change in 4.1 and 4.2) is the common certified baseline. The 2026 revision was scheduled for 16 September 2026 with an expected three-year transition (verify locally: publication, IAF dates, which edition the certificate cites). It is expected to keep the harmonized 4-10 structure and adds quality culture and ethics, separate risk and opportunity duties, stronger change management and fuller Annex A guidance.

## Clauses 4-10 and evidence to expect
- 4 Context: issues (4.1), interested parties (4.2), scope with justified non-applicable requirements (4.3), processes with owners, criteria and KPIs (4.4).
- 5 Leadership: commitment and customer focus, quality policy, roles and authorities.
- 6 Planning: risks and opportunities (6.1), measurable objectives with plans (6.2), planned changes (6.3).
- 7 Support: resources, calibrated and traceable measuring equipment (7.1.5), knowledge, competence, awareness, communication, documented information (7.5).
- 8 Operation: planning, customer requirements review, design and development (8.3), external providers (8.4), controlled production, traceability and change control (8.5), release (8.6), nonconforming outputs (8.7).
- 9 Performance: monitoring and customer satisfaction (9.1), internal audit (9.2), management review inputs and outputs (9.3).
- 10 Improvement: nonconformity and corrective action (10.2), continual improvement (10.3).

## Internal audit (ISO 19011:2018)
- Risk-based programme: frequency by process importance, changes and past results; auditors never audit their own work.
- Plan scope, criteria and sampling; open; gather evidence by interview, observation and records; trace real samples end to end; close; report; follow up.
- Finding = requirement (clause) + objective evidence (record ID, date, location) + statement of the gap. Grading follows certification practice (ISO/IEC 17021-1): major = requirement absent or broken down, or product conformity at risk; minor = isolated lapse. Opportunity for improvement: no requirement breached.

## Nonconformity and corrective action (10.2)
1. React: contain (quarantine, sort, stop shipment, tell the customer when product left), correct.
2. Find the root cause and check where else it can occur.
3. Act on the cause, then verify effectiveness with data over a stated period.
4. Update risks, FMEA, control plan and procedures; keep records.
Correction fixes the instance; corrective action removes the cause; 2015 replaced preventive action with risk-based planning in 6.1.

## 8D
D0 plan and emergency response; D1 team; D2 problem (5W2H, is/is-not); D3 interim containment with verification; D4 root cause for occurrence and for escape (why not detected), proven by switching the cause on and off; D5 choose and verify permanent actions; D6 implement and validate; D7 prevent recurrence systemically (FMEA, control plan, standards, read-across to similar products); D8 recognise the team. Timing follows the customer's CSR.

## Root cause tools
- 5 Whys: precise problem statement; evidence for each why; branch for several causes; ask three legs (why made, why not detected, why the system allowed it); stop at a cause you can control in the system.
- Ishikawa 6M: people, machine, method, material, measurement, environment; brainstorm, then test the likely causes with data.

## Output
Audit report (scope, criteria, findings table, conclusion) or CAPA/8D record with containment, verified root cause, actions with owner and date, and effectiveness evidence.

## Pitfalls
"Operator error, retrain" as root cause; closing CAPA without effectiveness data; audits that only read procedures; findings without evidence.

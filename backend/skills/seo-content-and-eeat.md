## When to use
Planning, briefing, writing, refreshing or auditing content meant to earn organic search traffic, including topic clusters and AI-assisted production.

## Method
1. Intent first: label each query informational, navigational, commercial investigation, transactional or local. From the SERP data given, note ranking formats and features (snippets, video, People also ask); match the dominant format and depth.
2. Keyword research: seed from customer language (calls, tickets, reviews), expand to variants and questions, then score volume, difficulty, business value (0-3), intent fit and current rank. Keywords that share a SERP go on one page; one primary keyword per URL prevents cannibalisation.
3. Topic clusters: a pillar page covers a core topic broadly; each cluster page covers one subtopic in depth. Pillar and clusters link both ways with descriptive anchors.
4. Brief per page: primary and secondary terms, intent, reader and job to be done, angle, the original value only you can add (own data, first-hand testing, expert input), H2 outline, internal links, author, reviewer, sources.
5. People-first test (Google helpful-content guidance): original information or analysis, more value than current results, clear sourcing, visible author expertise, no easily checked errors, a satisfied reader. Warning signs: made mainly for search visits, written to a word count, trends outside your audience, faked freshness by changing dates.
6. Who, How, Why: real bylines and bios where readers expect them; explain how content was produced, including automation or AI, when readers would wonder; the purpose is helping visitors.
7. E-E-A-T (rater guidelines of 11 September 2025): Experience (first-hand use), Expertise (knowledge or skill), Authoritativeness (a go-to source), Trust (the most important; an untrustworthy page has low E-E-A-T however expert it looks). Evidence comes from what the site says about itself, what independent sources say, and what the page shows. YMYL topics (health or safety, financial security, government, civics and society) need the highest accuracy and expert review. E-E-A-T is not itself a ranking factor and rater scores do not change rankings directly.
8. AI assistance is allowed when the result adds value and meets Search Essentials. Fact-check every claim, title, meta description, structured data item and alt text. Mass-producing low-value pages is scaled content abuse whatever the tool. Ecommerce AI images carry IPTC DigitalSourceType TrainedAlgorithmicMedia.

## Spam policies to avoid
Current policies (page updated 2026): cloaking, doorway abuse, expired domain abuse, hacked content, hidden text and link abuse, keyword stuffing, link spam (paid links need rel="sponsored" or rel="nofollow"), machine-generated traffic, malicious practices, misleading functionality, scaled content abuse, scraping, site reputation policy (third-party pages placed to borrow a host's ranking signals), sneaky redirects, thin affiliation, user-generated spam. Results: lower rankings or removal, possibly a manual action in Search Console; fix every instance, then file a reconsideration request.

## Checklist
- Intent and format match the SERP; one primary keyword per URL
- Original value named in the brief
- Author, reviewer (YMYL), sources and dates visible
- Every AI-drafted fact verified against a source

## Output
Keyword map (keyword, intent, volume, difficulty, value, target URL, status), content briefs, or a content audit deciding keep, update, merge, redirect or remove per URL with the evidence.

## Pitfalls
Fake authors or credentials; changing dates without real updates; thin programmatic pages with swapped place names; chasing volume with no business value; paraphrasing competitors with nothing added.

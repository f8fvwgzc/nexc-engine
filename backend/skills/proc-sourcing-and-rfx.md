## When to use
Designing a competitive sourcing event (RFI, RFP, RFQ, e-auction), building evaluation criteria and TCO models, or making an award that must survive audit or legal challenge.

## Choose the instrument
- RFI: market sounding on capability, capacity and approach; builds the long list; no commitment.
- RFP: outcome or partly defined need; suppliers propose solutions; quality and price evaluated.
- RFQ: complete specification; price and commercial terms compared; may lead to an e-auction.
- Staged: RFI -> shortlist -> RFP -> negotiation, where the rules allow.

## Process
1. Approved need, budget and category strategy.
2. Market analysis and pre-qualification: financial standing, insurance, certifications, ESG, sanctions and conflict checks.
3. Fix the evaluation model before release: criteria, sub-criteria, weights, 0-5 scale with descriptors, pass/fail gates, price model.
4. RFx pack: instructions, timetable, specification, pricing schedule, draft contract, evaluation method, clarification route.
5. Answer clarifications to all bidders equally and anonymised.
6. Evaluate: compliance -> independent scoring -> moderation with written rationale -> price (two-envelope: quality scored before price is seen) -> TCO.
7. Negotiate if permitted -> recommendation -> approval -> notification and debrief -> standstill (public) -> contract.

## Scoring
- Weighted score = sum of (weight_i x score_i), weights summing to 100%. Complex services often 60/40 to 80/20 quality/price; commodities price-heavy.
- Relative price score = lowest price / bid price x maximum points. Relative formulas can reverse rankings when a bid enters or leaves; absolute scoring against a budget or should-cost avoids this.
- Each score is justified against the descriptors in writing.

## TCO
TCO = acquisition (price, freight, duty, installation, training) + ownership (energy, maintenance, spares, quality failures, inventory carrying, administration) + end of life (disposal, exit and transition) - residual value. Discount multi-year flows to NPV at the company rate.

## E-auctions
Use only when the spec is complete, at least three qualified bidders compete, price is the main variable and the relationship can take it. Publish rules first: format (reverse English or sealed), reserve, decrement, extension rule, rank or price visibility. Avoid for strategic and bottleneck items.

## Fair and auditable award
Publish criteria and weights; conflict-of-interest declarations from every evaluator; no post-deadline changes for one bidder; log all contact; keep score sheets and moderation notes; test abnormally low bids against should-cost; record approvals.
- UK public bodies: Procurement Act 2023 (in force 24 February 2025) awards to the most advantageous tender; criteria must relate to the subject matter, be clear, measurable, specific and proportionate, with their relative importance stated; an apparently abnormally low tender may be set aside only after the supplier has had a fair chance to explain it; every supplier with an assessed tender gets an assessment summary; an 8 working day standstill normally follows the award notice.
- EU public bodies: Directive 2014/24/EU, most economically advantageous tender (MEAT).

## Output
RFx pack, evaluation model (criteria, weights, descriptors, gates), moderated score matrix with rationale, TCO table, award recommendation with approvals, and debrief or assessment summaries.

## Pitfalls
Changing criteria after bids arrive; weights that contradict stated priorities; price formulas that over-reward trivial savings; leaking bidder data; single-evaluator scoring; an RFQ for an undefined need.

## When to use
Writing or refreshing the audit charter, universe and annual plan, planning an engagement, or judging an independence or objectivity question.

## Standards
- IIA Global Internal Audit Standards: published 9 Jan 2024, effective 9 Jan 2025; 5 domains (I Purpose of Internal Auditing, II Ethics and Professionalism, III Governing the Internal Audit Function, IV Managing the Internal Audit Function, V Performing Internal Audit Services), 15 principles, 52 standards. Topical Requirements are mandatory for assurance on a topic that is the subject of a planned engagement, arises during one, or is requested later (advisory: recommended); each is effective 12 months after issue: Cybersecurity 5 Feb 2026, Third-Party 15 Sep 2026, Organizational Behavior 15 Dec 2026, Organizational Resilience 30 Apr 2027; more are in the pipeline, so check the IIA list.
- Key requirements: 2.2 objectivity (assurance over an activity you were responsible for within the previous 12 months is presumed impaired; 2.3 disclose impairments); 6.1-6.3 mandate, charter, board support; 7.1 confirm organisational independence to the board at least annually; 8.4 external quality assessment at least every five years with at least one active CIA on the team; 9.4 plan built on a documented assessment of strategies, objectives and risks, at least annually, covering IT governance, fraud and compliance and ethics, approved by the board; 9.5 coordination and reliance on other assurance providers; 11.5 risk accepted beyond appetite goes to senior management, then the board if unresolved.

## Building the plan
1. Universe: entities, processes, systems, projects, third parties, regulatory obligations; map each to strategic objectives and the risk register.
2. Score each unit 1-5 on weighted factors: financial size, complexity, regulatory exposure, pace of change, fraud susceptibility, IT dependence, prior issues, time since last audit, strength of other assurance.
3. Coordinate with second line and external audit; rely only on providers you judge competent and objective.
4. Cycle: high risk yearly, medium every 2-3 years, low by rotation or monitoring; add mandatory audits; hold roughly 10-20% contingency for emerging risks and advisory requests.
5. Resource the plan by skill and hours; co-source gaps; tell the board the impact of resource limits and why any high-risk area is left out.
6. Present: risk coverage map, schedule by quarter, budget, KPIs; get board approval; update when risks change.

## Engagement planning (Principle 13)
Kick-off communication with management (13.1); engagement risk assessment and risk and control matrix (13.2); objectives and scope, including period, locations, systems and exclusions (13.3); evaluation criteria: use management's own if adequate, otherwise set them through discussion with the board or senior management, e.g. policy, regulation, contract, COSO or COBIT (13.4); team and hours (13.5); work program with criteria, tasks, methods and staff, approved by the CAE before it is implemented and on any change (13.6).

## Independence and objectivity safeguards
Charter with unrestricted access; CAE reports functionally to the board and administratively to senior management; no operational duties; annual conflict declarations; staff rotation; extra review when assurance follows earlier advisory work on the same area.

## Output
Plan document: methodology, universe heat map, plan table (engagement, linked risk, assurance or advisory, quarter, hours), resources, coverage gaps, approval record. Engagement planning memo with RCM and work program.

## Pitfalls
Rolling last year's list forward; no link to strategy; missing IT and fraud coverage; hiding scope limits; auditors reviewing their own former work.

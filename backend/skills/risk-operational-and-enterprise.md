## When to use
Building or reviewing an ERM framework, appetite statement, risk register, RCSA, KRIs, loss data, scenario workshop or op risk capital estimate.

## Frameworks
- COSO ERM, Integrating with Strategy and Performance (2017): 5 components, 20 principles. Governance and Culture (board oversight, operating structures, desired culture, core values, capable people); Strategy and Objective-Setting (business context, risk appetite, alternative strategies, business objectives); Performance (identify, assess severity, prioritise, respond, portfolio view); Review and Revision (substantial change, review risk and performance, improve ERM); Information, Communication and Reporting (information and technology, communicate risk, report on risk, culture and performance).
- ISO 31000:2018 (revision in progress as ISO project 88574; check for a newer edition before citing). Principles: integrated, structured and comprehensive, customised, inclusive, dynamic, best available information, human and cultural factors, continual improvement. Framework: leadership and commitment; integration, design, implementation, evaluation, improvement. Process: communication and consultation; scope, context and criteria; assessment (identify, analyse, evaluate); treatment; monitoring and review; recording and reporting.
- Basel operational risk: seven event types (internal fraud; external fraud; employment practices and workplace safety; clients, products and business practices; damage to physical assets; business disruption and system failures; execution, delivery and process management). Standardised approach (OPE25) capital = BIC x ILM; BIC = BI x marginal 12% up to EUR 1bn, 15% for EUR 1-30bn, 18% above; loss component = 15 x average annual losses over 10 years (collection threshold EUR 20,000); ILM = ln(e - 1 + (LC/BIC)^0.8). Bucket 1 banks (BI up to EUR 1bn) use ILM = 1; supervisors may set ILM = 1 for all banks, so check the local rule.
- IIA Three Lines Model (Statement of Position, 8 Jul 2026, replacing the 2020 paper): management (first line) owns risks and runs processes and controls; second-line roles give expertise, monitoring and challenge; internal audit (third line) gives independent assurance, including over the second line, and advice; the board oversees.

## Risk appetite
Board-approved statement, qualitative and quantitative; tolerances and limits per risk type; KRIs with green, amber and red thresholds; escalation rules; cascade to business units.

## Tools
- Risk register fields: ID, cause-event-consequence statement, owner, category, inherent likelihood and impact (e.g. 5x5), key controls and effectiveness, residual rating, appetite status, response (avoid, reduce, share, accept), actions with owner and date, KRIs, review date.
- RCSA: map process, list risks and controls, self-assess design and operation, rate residual risk, raise actions; second line challenges; testing or audit validates.
- KRIs: leading or lagging, exact formula, data source, frequency, thresholds tied to appetite, owner (e.g. failed trades %, unpatched critical systems, staff turnover, overdue audit actions).
- Loss data: occurrence, discovery and accounting dates; gross loss, recoveries, event type, business line, root cause; near misses.
- Scenario analysis: severe but plausible events, structured workshop, frequency and severity estimates (e.g. 1-in-25, 1-in-100 years) anchored to internal and external losses; record biases.

## Output
Heat map and top risks vs appetite, register extract, KRI dashboard with breaches, loss trends and root causes, actions and owners.

## Pitfalls
Registers that list causes instead of risks; inherent and residual scores identical; KRIs with no thresholds; appetite never used in decisions; scenario numbers without evidence.

## When to use
Landing and product pages, ads, emails, CTAs and sales pages, and reviewing copy for clarity, persuasion and claim risk.

## Method
1. Research first: the reader's awareness stage (unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, most aware), their own words from reviews, interviews and tickets, objections, and the single action the page exists for.
2. One page, one goal, one primary CTA. Value proposition: for [segment] who [problem], [product] delivers [outcome], unlike [alternative], because [proof].
3. Pick a structure:
   - AIDA: Attention (headline), Interest (a relevant problem or fact), Desire (benefits plus proof), Action (CTA).
   - PAS: Problem, Agitate (the honest cost of doing nothing), Solution.
   - Before-After-Bridge; FAB: feature, advantage, benefit.
4. Features to benefits: keep asking "so what?" until you reach an outcome the reader values (time, money, risk, status); keep the feature as the proof.
5. Headlines: specific outcome, audience or number; test with the 4 U's (useful, urgent, unique, ultra-specific). Draft 10 or more and choose by clarity first; the subhead explains how.
6. CTAs: verb plus outcome ("Start my free trial", "Get the pricing guide"); put true friction reducers beside it (no card needed, cancel anytime).
7. Voice stays constant; tone adapts to context. Place the brand on four scales: formal-casual, serious-funny, respectful-irreverent, matter-of-fact-enthusiastic. List tone words and anti-words.
8. Readers scan: inverted pyramid, keywords up front, meaningful subheads, bullets, one idea per paragraph, plain words, no hype (marketese costs credibility).
9. Social proof: specific named testimonials with permission, customer logos, numbers with source and date, ratings with review counts.
10. Testing: one variable per A/B test, a primary metric fixed in advance, sample size computed before launch, full business cycles.

## Claims and disclosures (US FTC; name the jurisdiction for other markets)
- Substantiation: hold a reasonable basis for every objective claim, express or implied, before publishing. "Tests prove", "doctors recommend" or "studies show" promise that exact level of proof, so the tests, survey or studies must exist.
- Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255, revised 2023): disclose material connections (payment, free product, employment, family) clearly and conspicuously in the ad itself; if results are atypical, state generally expected results.
- Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465, final rule August 2024): no fake or AI-fabricated reviews or testimonials; no incentives conditioned on positive or negative sentiment; insider reviews disclose the connection; no company-run "independent" review sites; no suppressing reviews by threats; no buying or selling fake social-media indicators (bot or hijacked-account followers or views).
- Superlatives ("best", "#1", "guaranteed") need data or must be obvious puffery. In the EU apply the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive as amended by Directive (EU) 2019/2161; in the UK the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 bans fake reviews (verify locally).

## Checklist
- Offer clear within five seconds; one primary CTA
- Benefits lead, features prove; proof sits next to each claim
- Every claim in the claims register; disclosures visible near the claim

## Output
Copy deck: goal and audience, message hierarchy, 3+ headline options with rationale, body copy, CTA variants, claims register (claim, evidence, source, owner), test plan.

## Pitfalls
Clever over clear; fake urgency or scarcity; unsourced statistics; several competing CTAs; testimonials edited until they misrepresent the customer.

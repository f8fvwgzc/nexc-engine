## When to use
Onboarding, periodic or trigger reviews, ownership mapping, PEP files, EDD memos, or designing a customer risk model.

## Key standards
- FATF Recommendations (last amended June 2026): R.1 risk-based approach (February 2025 change: measures must be proportionate; simplified measures for proven lower risk), R.10 CDD, R.11 records kept at least 5 years, R.12 PEPs, R.13 correspondent banking, R.17 reliance on third parties, R.19 higher-risk countries, R.24 and R.25 ownership of legal persons and arrangements.
- US: FinCEN CDD Rule, 31 CFR 1010.230. Ownership prong: every individual owning 25% or more; control prong: one individual with significant control. FinCEN exceptive relief (13 February 2026): no need to re-identify and re-verify owners each time an existing legal-entity customer opens another account; still required at the first account, when facts cast doubt on the ownership data held, and as risk-based ongoing CDD requires.
- EU: AMLR, Regulation (EU) 2024/1624, applies from 10 July 2027; EU rules treat domestic and foreign PEPs alike and keep PEP measures at least 12 months after office ends.
- FATF call-for-action list as last checked (June 2026, verify locally): DPRK and Iran (countermeasures), Myanmar (EDD). It changes at each plenary (February, June, October).

## Method
1. Identify and verify: individuals (name, date of birth, address, ID number) against reliable independent documents or data; entities (legal name, registration number, legal form, address, directors, constitutional documents).
2. Ownership: trace every layer to natural persons and multiply through (60% of a holding that owns 50% = 30%). If nobody meets the threshold, name the senior managing official. Trusts: settlor, trustees, protector, beneficiaries or class, anyone with ultimate control.
3. Purpose and nature: expected products, volumes, counterparties, countries, source of funds.
4. Screen customer, owners and controllers for sanctions, PEP status and adverse media.
5. Risk-rate on customer, geography, product, channel (non-face-to-face) and expected activity; document the score, any override and who approved it.
6. EDD when high risk: senior management approval, evidenced source of wealth and source of funds, independent corroboration, tighter monitoring, shorter review cycle. Under FATF R.12 foreign PEPs always get EDD; domestic and international-organisation PEPs when risk is higher; family and close associates count as PEPs; leaving office does not end PEP risk automatically. If source of wealth cannot be explained, do not onboard.
7. Simplified due diligence only for documented low risk and never when suspicion exists.
8. Ongoing: review by risk (policy sets cycles, e.g. 1 year high, 2-3 medium, 3-5 low) and on triggers: ownership change, adverse news, unusual activity, alerts.
9. If CDD cannot be completed, do not open or continue the relationship and consider a suspicious activity report; never tip off.

## Output
CDD or EDD file: profile, ownership chart with percentages, screening results (list, date, disposition), risk rating with factors, SoW/SoF narrative and evidence, approvals, conditions, next review date, gaps.

## Pitfalls
Stopping at the first ownership layer; accepting nominees at face value; ignoring control without ownership; letting a score replace judgement; collecting more personal data than needed. Give general compliance analysis, name the jurisdiction, and leave legal conclusions to the MLRO or counsel.

## When to use
Qualifying inbound leads and pipeline, preparing or debriefing discovery calls, deal reviews, and pursue-or-walk-away decisions.

## Frameworks
- BANT: Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline. A quick first filter for inbound or transactional deals; weak in complex sales, where budget is often created once value is proven. Mark each qualified, partial or unknown and gather it over several conversations.
- MEDDICC (MEDDPICC adds Paper process):

| Element | Evidence you need |
|---|---|
| Metrics | a quantified outcome the buyer accepts (DSO down 12 days on 120 million revenue frees 120 x 12 / 365 = about 3.9 million cash) |
| Economic buyer | the person who can release funds and say yes alone; met or not |
| Decision criteria | technical, business and commercial criteria they will score |
| Decision process | steps, people and dates from evaluation to decision |
| Paper process | legal, procurement, security review, signature path, lead times |
| Implicate the pain | the business problem, its cost, who feels it, why now |
| Champion | power, influence and credibility, and sells for you when you are absent |
| Competition | other vendors, build in-house, budget elsewhere, do nothing |

Score each 0 (unknown), 1 (partial) or 2 (validated with evidence); every gap becomes a dated next action. Test a champion by asking for something that costs them effort, such as a meeting with the economic buyer or the evaluation scorecard. A friendly contact without power is a coach, not a champion.
- SPIN (Rackham; Huthwaite research on 35,000+ sales calls): Situation questions (facts; few, research them beforehand), Problem (difficulties, dissatisfaction), Implication (consequences, cost and spread of the problem, which builds urgency), Need-payoff (the buyer states the value of solving it). In large sales, success tracks turning implied needs into explicit needs.
- Challenger (teach, tailor, take control): open with an insight that reframes the buyer's problem and its cost, tailor the message to each stakeholder's metrics, and hold firm on value, next steps and process instead of conceding to keep the peace.

## Discovery call (30-45 minutes)
1. Upfront agreement: purpose, time, agenda, possible outcomes including "not a fit".
2. Context: confirm what research shows; only a few situation questions.
3. Pain: current state, problem, quantified impact, root cause, who else is affected.
4. Desired future state and how success will be measured.
5. Process: decision process and criteria, stakeholders, paper process, timing, budget source.
6. Next step: agreed, dated, named attendees; recap email the same day.
Let the buyer talk more than half the time; use open questions; summarise and confirm.

## Disqualify early
Nurture or walk away when there is no quantified pain or compelling event, no access to the economic buyer after asking, criteria written around a competitor, no budget path, a timeline beyond the planning horizon, or poor ICP fit. A fast no beats a slow no; record the reason.

## Output
Qualification scorecard (element, evidence, score, gap, next action, owner, date), call plan with questions, call summary.

## Pitfalls
Interrogation-style checklists; single-threading through one contact; accepting "budget TBD" forever; leading questions; advancing stages on optimism instead of evidence.

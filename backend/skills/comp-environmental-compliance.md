## When to use
Declaring substance compliance of EEE, BOM substance reviews, supplier declarations, battery, packaging and take-back obligations, conflict minerals reports.

## RoHS (EU)
- Directive 2011/65/EU, Annex II as amended by (EU) 2015/863: Pb, Hg, Cr(VI), PBB, PBDE and phthalates DEHP, BBP, DBP, DIBP at most 0.1 % w/w; Cd at most 0.01 %. Limits apply per homogeneous material, not per part.
- Phthalates apply since 22 July 2019 (22 July 2021 for medical devices and monitoring and control instruments). Open scope covers all EEE not excluded.
- Exemptions (Annex III, Annex IV for categories 8 and 9) are time-limited: check expiry and renewal status and cite the number used.
- Evidence: technical documentation to EN IEC 63000:2018, supplier declarations, risk-based testing (XRF screening, IEC 62321 methods). RoHS is part of the CE marking.

## REACH (Regulation (EC) 1907/2006)
- Candidate List SVHC (updated about twice a year): Art. 33 duty to inform recipients when an article holds more than 0.1 % w/w, judged per article including each component article (CJEU C-106/14); consumers on request within 45 days.
- Art. 7(2): notify ECHA when the SVHC is above 0.1 % and above 1 t/year per producer or importer.
- SCIP database (Waste Framework Directive Art. 9(1)(i)): notify articles with an SVHC above 0.1 %, mandatory since 5 January 2021.
- Also Annex XVII restrictions and the POPs Regulation (EU) 2019/1021.

## WEEE (2012/19/EU)
Register as producer in each Member State of sale (authorised representative for distance sellers), finance take-back (EPR), crossed-out wheeled-bin symbol (EN 50419) and producer mark, report quantities. Member State collection target: 65 % of the average weight of EEE placed on the market in the three preceding years, or 85 % of WEEE generated. The Commission published its evaluation in July 2025; watch for a revision.

## Batteries (Regulation (EU) 2023/1542)
- Applies from 18 February 2024. CE marking and conformity assessment from 18 August 2024; labelling from 18 August 2026; QR code and battery passport (EV, LMT, industrial above 2 kWh) from 18 February 2027.
- Portable batteries removable and replaceable by the end user from 18 February 2027 (Art. 11, limited derogations).
- Substance limits: Hg 0.0005 %, Cd 0.002 % (portable), Pb 0.01 % (portable). Due-diligence duties postponed to 2027 (verify date locally). Producer EPR registration per Member State.

## Packaging (PPWR, Regulation (EU) 2025/40)
In force 11 February 2025, applies from 12 August 2026, replacing 94/62/EC. PFAS limits for food-contact packaging, recyclability by 2030, minimisation and empty-space limits, harmonised labelling and EPR. Several duties phase in later; verify each date.

## Conflict minerals
EU Regulation (EU) 2017/821 (since 1 January 2021) binds Union importers of 3TG ores and metals (Annex I codes, above volume thresholds) to OECD five-step due diligence; it does not cover components or finished products. US SEC Rule 13p-1: Form SD by 31 May. Collect RMI CMRT (3TG) and EMRT (cobalt, mica and more) from suppliers; check smelters against RMAP status.

## Method and output
1. Collect full material declarations (IPC-1752, current revision, or IEC 62474 format) or at least RoHS/REACH statements per MPN.
2. Find gaps: missing or stale declarations, expired exemptions, SVHC hits after the latest list update.
3. Test high-risk parts (cables, plastics, solder, coatings).
4. Output matrix: MPN | supplier | RoHS status and exemption | SVHC, CAS, % w/w | SCIP number | battery or packaging duty | evidence date.

## Pitfalls
Weighting by whole part instead of homogeneous material; declarations older than the current Candidate List; forgetting batteries and packaging are separate regimes; relying on an expired exemption.
